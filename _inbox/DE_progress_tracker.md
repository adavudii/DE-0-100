## Tips for Becoming a Genuinely Strong DE (not just "syllabus-complete")

1. **Break things on purpose.** Once code works, intentionally feed it bad data (empty CSV, wrong data type, missing column) and see what breaks. Real pipelines fail constantly — knowing *why* is more valuable than knowing the happy path.

2. **Read error messages fully, every time.** You're already doing this well (the TypeError moment) — keep it up. Most beginners panic and Google the first 3 words instead of reading what Python is actually telling them.

3. **Rebuild without looking, after a few days.** After finishing a concept, wait 2-3 days, then try rebuilding a small version from memory before checking notes. This is what actually moves things from "I did this once" to "I know this."

4. **Comment your code like you're teaching someone.** You're already planning to teach this later — write comments now as if a beginner is reading your code. Free content, and it forces you to actually understand what you wrote.

5. **Every project gets a README, even tiny ones.** Not just the big milestones — even a 10-line script. This builds the habit before it matters, and gives you a body of small, visible proof-of-work.

6. **Don't skip the "boring" command line and Git practice.** These are the things that separate "can follow a tutorial" from "can actually work on a team." Recruiters and interviewers notice this fast.

7. **Your trading background is a genuine edge — use it early.** Most self-taught DE portfolios look identical (generic Kaggle datasets). Yours won't, because of the crypto angle. Start hinting at this even in Phase 0/1 posts, not just at Phase 5.

8. **Log confusion, not just wins.** The "debugging tips" section in your notes is gold for future content and for interviews — "tell me about a time you were stuck" is a real interview question, and you'll have real, honest examples.
